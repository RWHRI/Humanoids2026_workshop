Long-Term Human-Humanoid Co-Adaptation

3rd Workshop on Real-World Physical and Social Human-Robot Interaction in the 2026 IEEE-RAS 25th International Conference on Humanoid Robots (Humanoids), 6th December 2026, California, USA.

The website for the first edition of this workshop at IEEE-Humanoids 2024, featuring proceedings, accepted papers, and a picture gallery, can be accessed at: https://rwhri.github.io/Humanoids2024_workshop/

The website for the second edition of this workshop at IEEE-Humanoids 2025, featuring proceedings, accepted papers, and a picture gallery, can be accessed at: https://rwhri.github.io/Humanoids2025_workshop/

The workshop will take place in a hybrid mode and available for online participants via Teams: TBD

With the increasing pace of innovation in humanoid robotics, it is important to take stock of current achievements and what could realistically come next.Thanks to their physical capabilities, humanoid robots have the potential to assist humans in a wide range of scenarios - homes, hospitals, public spaces, and workplaces - yet significant technological advances are still required. Early Human–Robot Interaction (HRI) systems often relied on limited, single-modality interactions.Today, however, there is a growing expectation for richer, more natural, and even personalized forms of interaction. Humanoid robots must not only perceive and interpret diverse human cues, but also respond in ways that are context-aware, socially intelligent, and acceptable to users. Achieving this requires moving beyond short-term interaction toward long-term human–robot co-adaptation. To this end, robotic systems must incorporate mechanisms to recognize and overcome their own limitations, continuously adapting their behavior to users and learning from natural human inputs. These challenges become even more pronounced outside controlled environments, where variability and unpredictability inhuman behavior are the norm. ​ This third edition of our Human-Robot Interaction workshop focuses on the key challenges that currently limit the adoption of humanoid technologies in real-world applications. In particular, we aim to foster discussion on how robots can represent and understand their own skills, and model human behavior and state. From these foundations, mutual adaptations is the follow-up, with learning from the human guidance the next step to overcome their limits. From the user perspective, interactions must be felt as natural and intuitive as possible, personalizable, and they must address the ethical and societal implications associated with long-term human–robot coexistence.​ We invite speakers from both academia and industry recognizing their complementary roles: academia in pushing the boundaries of research, and industry in translating these advances into robust real-world systems. Special attention will be given to adaptive behaviors in humanoids, robot self-awareness, online and incremental learning, personalized interaction, shared autonomy, and cognitive and behavioral modeling of humans. A central goal is to advance the design of user-centered interfaces that can adapt over time to diverse users and contexts. The workshop will feature keynote talks by established researchers from academia and industry, an interactive panel discussion to actively engage the audience, and contributions from emerging researchers through short talks and a poster session. We aim to foster dialogue across disciplines and experience levels to better understand current trends and future challenges. A dedicated website will provide all relevant information, and workshop contributions will be made publicly available to maximize visibility and impact:

  • Physical and social human-humanoids interaction.
  • Long-term human-robot co-adaptation and mutual learning.
  • Assistive flexible and long-term robotic assistance technologies
  • Embodied intelligence and adaptive behavior in humanoids
  • Robot self-awareness of physical and social interaction skills
  • Online and incremental learning for robot skill acquisition from human interventions
  • Personalized human–robot interaction based on user preferences, abilities, and intent
  • Shared autonomy and collaborative decision-making
  • Cognitive and behavioral modeling of humans in interaction loops
  • Real-world applications of physical and social HRI with a focus on the mentioned topics
  • Ethical, societal, and legal implications of long-term human–robot interactions

Call for Contributions

We invite short paper submissions presenting late-breaking results, novel ideas, design case studies, or empirical findings on real-world physical and social human-robot interaction with humanoids. Accepted authors will receive ~5 minutes for spotlight presentations and participate in a 40-minute interactive poster session, engaging with an expected audience of 50–60 participants, including leading experts from academia and industry. Submit your work for a valuable opportunity to share, discuss, and collaborate on the key challenges and advances shaping the future of Human-Humanoids Robot Interaction.
Date: October 2, 2025 (Full-day workshop)
Location: Hybrid (Seoul, South Korea and online), as part of The 2025 IEEE-RAS International Conference on Humanoid Robots, IEEE-Humanoids 2025.
Submission Instruction: Email your contributions to: whsop.realworld.hri@gmail.com
Important: Special Session in a IEEE-RAS Journal! We are currently in the process of submitting a proposal for a dedicated special issue in a related IEEE-Robotics and Automation Society journal (e.g. RA-L), focusing on advances in physical and social human-humanoid interaction, multimodal perception, adaptive communication, and real-world applications with humanoid robots, reflecting the key themes of the workshop. All workshop contributions will be invited to submit extended versions of their work for consideration in this special issue. If you are a researcher interested in contributing to this special issue, please contact us for more information.
Contact for submissions: paragk@kth.se, davide.torielli@iit.it.

SUBMISSION GUIDELINES

Manuscripts should be written in English and will undergo a single-blind review by the organizing committee and selected researchers from the field. The length should be 2-4 pages excluding references. We welcome contributions that include work in progress, preliminary results, technical reports, case studies, surveys, and state-of-the-art research. Position papers are also welcome and should be at least 2 pages excluding references. These can be research project proposals or plans without results.
