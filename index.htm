Long-Term Human-Humanoid Co-Adaptation

3rd Workshop on Real-World Physical and Social Human-Robot Interaction in the 2026 IEEE-RAS 25th International Conference on Humanoid Robots (Humanoids), 6th December 2026, California, USA.

The website for the first edition of this workshop at IEEE-Humanoids 2024, featuring proceedings, accepted papers, and a picture gallery, can be accessed at: https://rwhri.github.io/Humanoids2024_workshop/

The website for the second edition of this workshop at IEEE-Humanoids 2025, featuring proceedings, accepted papers, and a picture gallery, can be accessed at: https://rwhri.github.io/Humanoids2025_workshop/

The workshop will take place in a hybrid mode and available for online participants via Teams: TBD

Thanks to their physical capabilities, humanoid robots have the potential to assist humans in a wide range of scenarios - homes, hospitals, public spaces, and workplaces. Today, there is a growing expectation for richer, more natural, and even personalized forms of interaction. Humanoid robots must not only perceive and interpret diverse human cues, but also respond in ways that are context-aware, socially intelligent, and acceptable to users. Achieving this requires moving beyond short-term interaction toward long-term human-robot co-adaptation. To this end, robotic systems must incorporate mechanisms to recognize and overcome their own limitations, continuously adapting their behavior to users and learning from natural human inputs. This third edition of our Human-Robot Interaction workshop aims to foster discussion on how robots can represent and understand their own skills, and model human behavior and state. From these foundations, mutual adaptation is the follow-up, with learning from human guidance the next step to overcome their limits. From the user perspective, interactions must be felt as natural and intuitive as possible, personalizable, and they must address the ethical and societal implications associated with long-term human-robot coexistence. Special attention will be given to adaptive behaviors in humanoids, robot self-awareness, online and incremental learning, personalized interaction, shared autonomy, and cognitive and behavioral modeling of humans. The workshop will feature keynote talks by established researchers from academia and industry, an interactive panel discussion to actively engage the audience, and contributions from emerging researchers through short talks and a poster session.

  • Physical and social human-humanoids interaction.
  • Long-term human-robot co-adaptation and mutual learning.
  • Assistive flexible and long-term robotic assistance technologies
  • Embodied intelligence and adaptive behavior in humanoids
  • Robot self-awareness of physical and social interaction skills
  • Online and incremental learning for robot skill acquisition from human interventions
  • Personalized human-robot interaction based on user preferences, abilities, and intent
  • Shared autonomy and collaborative decision-making
  • Cognitive and behavioral modeling of humans in interaction loops
  • Real-world applications of physical and social HRI with a focus on the mentioned topics
  • Ethical, societal, and legal implications of long-term human–robot interactions

Call for Contributions

We invite short paper submissions presenting late-breaking results, novel ideas, design case studies, or empirical findings on real-world physical and social human-robot interaction with humanoids. Accepted authors will receive ~5 minutes for spotlight presentations and participate in the interactive poster session, Submit your work for a valuable opportunity to share, discuss, and collaborate on the key challenges and advances shaping the future of Human Robot Interaction.

Important Dates

  • Submissions startOctober 13, 2026
  • Submission deadlineNovember 16, 2026
  • Notification of acceptanceNovember 20, 2026
  • Presentation and Poster SessionDecember 7, 2026

🕑 All deadlines are at 23:59 Anywhere on Earth (AoE).

Submission Guidelines

Manuscripts should be written in English and will undergo a single-blind review by the organizing committee. The length should be 2-4 pages, excluding references. We welcome contributions that include work in progress, preliminary results, technical reports, project proposals, case studies, surveys, and state-of-the-art research.

Templates: Authors must use the templates provided by the main conference:

  • LaTeX Template
  • Word Template

Email your contributions to whsop.realworld.hri@gmail.com .
Any questions about submissions can be sent to d.torielli@tudelft.nl, damiano.gasperini@iit.it, paragk@kth.se,

Presentation requirement: At least one author of each accepted contribution will be asked to present their work through a short talk (duration ~5min) and a poster during the dedicated poster-session timeslot.
